## What it does

The Library is where you review and govern everything Pluvo has learned about your data: what your columns mean, how your tables relate, how your metrics are calculated, and which queries are trusted.

Approval is what makes a definition count. Only approved definitions that compile successfully are ever used to answer questions, so anything still suggested, rejected, disabled, or broken is never treated as authoritative. That is what keeps results consistent and defensible.

## What it holds

Five kinds of definition:

- **Metrics**, named measures with their computation and filters
- **Column meanings**, what a specific column represents and how it should be treated
- **Relationships**, how two tables join together, with match quality
- **Value maps**, how values in one system correspond to values in another
- **Saved queries**, approved queries for a specific business question

## Getting around

The Library has its own navigation down the left.

**Overview** is where you land. It gives you the state of your library at a glance: how much of your data is covered, how many definitions are approved, how many are waiting on review, and how many have broken. Below that, **Needs attention** lists the things worth handling now, and **Recent activity** shows who approved, rejected, or suggested what, including the reason given for a rejection.

**Review** is your queue of decisions, with a count of what is waiting.

**Browse** lets you work through everything, either all definitions at once or one kind at a time, with filters for status, source, and connection. Rejected and disabled definitions are tucked away by default, and **Show dismissed** brings them back into view.

The search box at the top searches your whole library and drops you into Browse with the results filtered.

## The review inbox

**Review** is split into three tabs.

**Suggestions** are new definitions waiting for a person to decide. Work down the list and approve or reject each one. You can move with the arrow keys, press **A** to approve, and **R** to reject.

**Drift breaks** are metrics that stopped matching your data. A metric lands here when its numbers no longer reconcile, or when another metric it depends on was un-approved. Either way it is excluded from answers until you look at it. This is the one to watch: it is how you find out something quietly stopped being trustworthy.

**Conflicts** are questions people actually asked that Pluvo could not answer from your approved definitions. Each one shows the question, whether Pluvo declined to answer or answered without verified figures, and how many times it has come up. They are a useful reading of what your library is still missing.

<Note>
  The review inbox covers metrics and saved queries. To review suggested column meanings, relationships, or value maps, go to **Browse** and filter the status to Suggested.
</Note>

## Making a decision

**Approve** puts a definition to work. **Reject** marks it wrong, and Pluvo will not suggest it again. **Disable** takes something out of use without saying it was wrong.

Rejecting asks for an optional reason. It is worth writing one, because it appears alongside the decision later and saves the next person wondering why.

All three decisions are reversible. Click an applied decision again to undo it, and the definition returns to suggested.

## Reading a metric

Open any metric and you get the full picture.

**How it's computed** explains the calculation in plain language, step by step, such as summing a column on a table, converting currency at each row's rate, or filtering to particular values. The compiled expression sits underneath if you want the precise version.

**What it means** is the description, along with any guidance that shapes how Pluvo uses it.

**Source and lineage** shows which connection, tables, and joins it draws on, and what role each column plays.

**Governance** records its status, where it came from, who approved it, who added it, and its version.

**Reconciled to ground truth** is the trust check. Metrics are reconciled against an expected value, and this panel shows the measured value, the expected value, the difference, and when it was last checked, along with whether it sits within tolerance. A metric needs that reconciliation before it can be approved, which is why an unreconciled metric cannot be put to work yet.

## Previewing the data

Metrics and saved queries have a **Run query** button that returns the actual rows behind the definition. You can sort and filter across the full result and see per-column totals, which is the quickest way to catch a definition that looks right but is not.

## Where definitions come from

You do not write definitions by hand. They arrive as Pluvo learns from your connected data, from a catalog of common definitions, or when someone promotes a query out of an Assistant conversation.

That last route is worth knowing about. When the Assistant produces a figure you trust, you can save its query to the Library so future answers reuse the same approved logic. Saved that way it arrives already approved, since choosing to save it is the approval. Answers that draw on Library content are labelled, so you can see they rest on definitions you govern.

## Tips

<Tip>
  Start on **Overview** and work the **Needs attention** list. It puts the highest-value decisions in front of you instead of leaving you to hunt through everything.
</Tip>

<Tip>
  Read the **Conflicts** tab now and then. It tells you what your team is trying to ask and cannot yet get a grounded answer to, which is the best guide to what to define next.
</Tip>

<Note>
  On a definition's detail page, the "Where it's used" and "History" tabs are not available yet.
</Note>
