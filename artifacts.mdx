## What it does

An artifact is something the Assistant builds for you from your data: a self-contained, interactive page you can keep, share, refresh, and let other people click through. Rather than an answer that scrolls away in a conversation, it is a finished piece of work, with the query behind every figure attached.

Each artifact is one file with everything it needs baked in, so you can also download it and open it anywhere, even offline.

## The four kinds

Artifacts come in four kinds, and each has its own page in the sidebar:

| | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| **Dashboards** | Key metrics at a glance, kept up to date |
| **Reports** | Written narratives, with every number traceable to source |
| **Presentations** | A deck to walk your audience through the numbers |
| **Canvas** | A blank space to build almost anything else |

The Assistant decides which kind it is building based on what you ask for, and files it on the matching page. All four work the same way once they exist, so everything below applies to each of them.

<Note>
  Artifacts created before the four kinds were introduced were filed under **Canvas**, so look there for your older work.
</Note>

## Creating an artifact

Artifacts are built by the Assistant, and asking for one directly is what triggers it. The easiest starting points are the buttons on the Assistant's home screen, **Create a dashboard**, **Create a report**, and **Create a presentation**, or **Create with AI** on any of the four pages.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ask for what you want">
    Describe it plainly, for example "Build an interactive dashboard of Q1 spend by department" or "Create a board presentation of our Q3 results."
  </Step>
  <Step title="Answer a few questions">
    If your request leaves room for interpretation, the Assistant asks a question or two first, with options to click, such as the period, the audience, or the look. A specific request skips straight to building.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It gets built and saved">
    The Assistant queries the data it needs, builds the artifact, and saves it. It opens in a panel beside your conversation, and it is filed on the right page in the sidebar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open it on its own">
    Use **Full page** from the panel, or find it later on its page in the sidebar.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Checking the numbers

Artifacts carry their provenance with them, so you can interrogate any figure without leaving the page.

Figures traced back to a query appear with a **dotted underline**. Click one to see its value, what it measures, the source it came from, the period, how it was calculated, and when the data was last synced. Expand **View query** to see the exact query that produced it. Hover any table or chart for **Source**, which opens the data behind it, and **CSV**, which downloads it.

Figures can also come from a spreadsheet. Those show the spreadsheet, the cell, and the formula they came from, with a link to open it.

<Tip>
  Not every number carries a dotted underline. Pluvo marks what it can trace and leaves the rest plain, so an unmarked figure means it was not traced, rather than that it is wrong.
</Tip>

## Keeping the numbers current

An artifact holds the figures from when it was built or last refreshed, and it does not change on its own. When you need current numbers, choose **Refresh data**.

How a refresh works depends on the kind of artifact.

**Dashboards** refresh quickly. Every figure, table, and chart is drawn straight from the query behind it, so a refresh simply re-runs those queries and redraws the page. Because the page itself does not change, a dashboard refresh does not add a version to History.

**Reports, presentations, and canvases** take a few minutes. A refresh re-runs the queries and updates the figures along with the wording around them, so the two still agree, and it adds a new version to History. Anything you supplied yourself, or that was stated as an assumption, carries over untouched.

Either way, the previous version stays on screen until the refresh succeeds, so a failed refresh never leaves you with a half-updated page.

<Note>
  Refreshing needs connected data to draw from. If your organization has no data connected, Pluvo tells you why the refresh could not run.
</Note>

## Editing an artifact

Choose **Edit** on the artifact, then pick how you want to work.

**Edit with AI** opens the Assistant beside the artifact, picking up that artifact's own conversation. Describe the change, for example "change the Q3 column to show margin instead," and the Assistant edits it in place. **Edit a section** lets you click the exact part you mean first.

**Edit text** lets you change the wording yourself, directly on the page. Click any outlined passage or list to edit it, format with bold, italic, bullets, or numbers, and add or delete blocks. Undo works as you would expect. When you are done, choose **Save**.

<Note>
  Figures that come from your data cannot be typed over, which keeps every number traceable. You can delete one, or type @ to insert a figure from the artifact. Amounts you type yourself are underlined, as a reminder that they are not linked to your data.
</Note>

### Refreshing text you wrote yourself

If you have edited wording by hand, **Refresh data** asks what to do with it:

- **Rewrite them with the new data** updates your edited text along with the rest. Your version stays in History.
- **Keep my words** updates only the numbers inside your text, then flags those passages so you can check the words still fit.

The Assistant also leaves wording you wrote alone unless you specifically ask it to change it.

To ask about an artifact rather than change it, choose **Ask Assistant**. Anyone who can view the artifact can use it.

## Changing the look

Every new artifact is styled to look polished in both light and dark mode. To change how it looks, ask the Assistant in plain words: a colour, a font, the title size, rounded or square corners, the chart colours, or the cover. Ask it to reset the look to undo your changes.

Dashboards, presentations, and canvases can also take a fully custom theme, so you can describe the style you want.

### Brand kits

A brand kit is your organization's house style, saved in the **Library** as a skill or rule. When one applies to you, the Assistant uses it for every new artifact automatically. If several apply, it asks which one to use, and you can always ask for the standard Pluvo look instead.

An artifact keeps the kit it was built with, so updating a brand kit later does not restyle artifacts you have already made.

## Version history

Every build, edit, refresh, and restore is kept.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open History">
    Choose **History** from the artifact's **⋯** menu. Each version shows what changed, such as **Created**, **Edited**, **Section edited**, **Refreshed**, **Look changed**, or **Restored**, along with when it happened.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Preview an earlier version">
    Choose **Preview** to load it read-only. A banner tells you which version you are viewing, and **Back to latest** returns you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Restore it if you want it back">
    Choose **Restore** and confirm. Restoring does not erase anything, it adds a new version recording what you restored from.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Templates

When you have built something worth repeating, such as a monthly dashboard or a board deck, turn it into a template. A template keeps the structure, headings, and styling, but none of the numbers, so every time someone uses it, Pluvo builds a fresh version from current data.

Dashboards, reports, and presentations can become templates. Canvases cannot.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Save it as a template">
    Choose **Save as template** from the artifact's **⋯** menu. The Assistant opens beside it and asks a few questions, one at a time: whether the template should always use the same data or ask each time, how the period should be chosen, anything else that changes between uses such as entity or department, and what to call it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    The Assistant summarizes what it will save. Choose **Yes, save it**, or **No, I have changes** to adjust.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Share it">
    A new template is private to you. To let others use it, choose **Share** and turn on **Everyone in your organization can use it**, or add specific people.
  </Step>
</Steps>

To use a template, open it from the **Templates** tab and choose **Use template**. The Assistant builds a new version from your current data. A dashboard is rebuilt as an exact copy with its queries updated, while a report or presentation shows you a preview first and asks before building.

## Sharing an artifact

Choose **Share**, add people from your organization, and pick whether each can view or edit. They get a notification and an email with a link.

People with edit access can change the artifact, refresh it, and restore earlier versions. Viewers can read it and ask the Assistant about it.

## Finding and managing artifacts

Each of the four pages has a tab for your own work, such as **Your dashboards**, alongside **Templates**. The list shows each artifact's name, status, who created it, who it is shared with, and when it was last updated.

Status tells you where an artifact stands: **Ready**, **Refreshing** while a refresh is running, or **Failed**. You can search by name, filter by status or by who created it, and sort by name or last updated.

Across the top of an artifact you will find **Ask Assistant**, **Edit**, **Refresh data**, and **Share**. Everything else lives in its **⋯** menu:

- **History**, to see and restore earlier versions
- **Download HTML**, to save it as a single file you can open outside Pluvo
- **Rename**
- **Save as template**
- **Delete**

Deleting is permanent, so ask the Assistant to rebuild it if you need it back.

## Tips

<Tip>
  Artifacts work best when someone else will want to explore the numbers themselves rather than just read your conclusion. Build one when you want to hand over the exploration, not only the answer.
</Tip>

<Tip>
  Before you share an artifact widely, open **History** from the **⋯** menu and check you are handing over the version you mean.
</Tip>
