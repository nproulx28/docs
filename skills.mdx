## What it does

A skill is a standing instruction for Pluvo: how a metric is calculated, what counts and what does not, a pitfall to steer around, or context about your organization to always keep in mind.

The problem it solves is repetition. Instead of explaining in every conversation that revenue means recognized revenue net of refunds, or that intercompany accounts are excluded from rollups, you write it down once and Pluvo applies it going forward.

Skills are written by your team. Nothing runs when you save one, it becomes reference knowledge that Pluvo brings into its work when the question calls for it.

A skill applies to you once you switch it on. For instructions that must apply to everyone on every answer, your admins use **Rules** instead.

## Writing a skill

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start a new skill">
    Open **Skills** and choose **New skill**. You get an authoring chat on the left and the skill itself on the right.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Describe what Pluvo should know">
    Tell the assistant what you want to teach it, such as a definition, a rule, or a pitfall in your data. It interviews you a question at a time and fills in a draft as you go. You can also type directly into the editor yourself.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review what it proposes">
    When the assistant proposes wording, you see what changed and can **Accept** or **Reject** it. Nothing is written into your skill without your say.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name it">
    Give it a clear **Name** and write the **Instructions**. Markdown is supported, up to 10,000 characters.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Activate it">
    Choose **Activate** to put it to work. Drafts are saved as you type but are not used until they are active.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  A good skill states the rule, the pitfall, and the boundary. For example: when an analysis involves revenue, revenue means recognized revenue net of refunds and never bookings, and intercompany accounts are excluded from all rollups.
</Tip>

## Turning skills on for yourself

Every skill has an **Applied to me** switch, and it starts off. A skill only affects your work once you switch it on, and even then Pluvo brings it in when it is relevant to the question rather than every time.

That means a skill someone else wrote sits waiting until you choose to use it, so you can keep your own set focused.

## Sharing a skill

A skill you write, or one the assistant saves for you, is private to you to begin with. Choose **Share** to make it available to the whole organization, or to give specific people access.

<Tip>
  If you find yourself explaining the same thing to Pluvo in several conversations, ask the assistant to save it as a skill. It only does this when you ask, and what it saves is private to you until you share it.
</Tip>

## Managing your skills

The **Skills** list shows each skill with a short excerpt, whether it is **Active** or a **Draft**, whether it is applied to you, and when it was last updated and by whom. You can search skills and filter by status.

Click a skill to read it in full and adjust its settings, or use its menu to **Edit skill**, **Deactivate** or **Activate** it, **Share** it, or **Delete skill**. Deleting is permanent and Pluvo stops using the skill immediately.

## Skills, Rules, and Library

Three things shape how Pluvo answers, and they do different jobs:

- **Skills** are instructions you write and opt into, used when they are relevant.
- **Rules** are standing instructions your admins set, applied to every answer for everyone.
- **Library** is the structural knowledge about your data, including the metrics and tables your figures are calculated from.

Use skills for guidance that helps in context, rules for what must hold every time, and the Library for definitions.
