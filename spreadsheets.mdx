## What it does

Spreadsheets gives you a familiar grid for building models without leaving Pluvo. It works the way you expect a spreadsheet to: cells, formulas, multiple sheets, and a ribbon toolbar for formatting, filtering, sorting, and the rest.

Start from a blank sheet, import a workbook you already have, such as an existing FP&A model, or ask the Assistant to build one for you.

<Note>
  Cells you type or paste are ordinary values. To keep a sheet linked to your data, add a **live sheet**, which fills itself from a saved Table in your Library and refreshes on demand.
</Note>

## Creating a spreadsheet

Open **Spreadsheets** from the sidebar, then choose how to start.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start blank">
    Choose **New spreadsheet**. You get an empty workbook with one sheet, ready to build in.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Or import a workbook">
    Choose **Import .xlsx** and pick an Excel file in `.xlsx` format, up to 25 MB. Older `.xls` files need saving as `.xlsx` first. The spreadsheet takes the file's name.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You can also ask the Assistant in any chat to create a spreadsheet for you. It asks for a title, builds it, and links to it in the answer.

### What comes across when you import

Importing keeps the structure, logic, and look of your workbook: values, formulas, number formats, merged cells, column widths, row heights, sheet names, and hidden sheets, along with cell styling such as fonts, fills, borders, and alignment. Exporting writes the same styling back out, so a model keeps its formatting on the way in and the way out.

A few things do not carry over, and Pluvo tells you which when the import finishes. Defined names, cell comments, and filters may be left behind.

<Tip>
  If a workbook is over 25 MB, split it into smaller files first. Removing unused sheets and ranges is often enough.
</Tip>

## Working in a spreadsheet

Edit cells and write formulas as you would in any spreadsheet, and add sheets as you need them. The toolbar covers formatting, filtering, sorting, find and replace, data validation, and conditional formatting.

Your work saves automatically a couple of seconds after you stop typing, and whenever you switch away. You can also press Cmd+S or Ctrl+S, or choose **Save**. The status beside it tells you whether everything is **Saved** or you have **Unsaved changes**.

## Working with the Assistant in a spreadsheet

Choose **Assistant** in the top bar to open a chat beside the sheet. It sees the sheet you are on and the cells you have selected, so you can ask about the numbers in front of you, or ask it to make changes. To point it at a specific range, select the cells, right-click, and choose **Assistant**.

The Assistant can read and explain what is in the workbook, and it can edit it for you: enter values and formulas, apply number formats and styling, merge cells, resize and insert rows and columns, and add or rename sheets. While it works, the sheet is locked so your edits and its edits do not collide.

<Tip>
  Select the range you care about before you ask. "Add a year-on-year growth column next to this" is much easier for the Assistant to get right when it can see exactly which cells you mean.
</Tip>

Chats with the spreadsheet Assistant are saved like any other, and **Past conversations** in the panel brings them back.

<Warning>
  Spreadsheets do not support editing by several people at once. If two people edit the same spreadsheet at the same time, the last save wins, so agree who is working in it before you start.
</Warning>

## Live sheets

A live sheet is a sheet filled straight from one of your approved Tables in the **Library**, so the numbers stay tied to your data instead of being pasted in. Build your model on the other sheets and point formulas at the live one.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Live data">
    Choose **Live data** in the top bar of the spreadsheet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a Table">
    Choose **Add live sheet** and search your approved saved Tables. If the Table has inputs, such as a period or entity, choose the values this sheet should be filled with.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use it in your model">
    Pluvo adds a new sheet with the Table's data. Its tab is blue, and its cells cannot be typed over, so the data always matches the source.
  </Step>
</Steps>

From **Live data** you can manage each live sheet:

- **Refresh** pulls the latest data into the sheet.
- **Change inputs** lets you pick different values, then **Apply and refresh**.
- **Freeze** keeps the current values and stops refreshing, for example to close a month. A frozen sheet's tab turns grey, and **Unfreeze** brings it back to life.
- **Convert to a normal sheet** stops syncing and keeps the current data as ordinary, editable cells.
- **Delete this sheet** removes it from the spreadsheet.

<Note>
  A live sheet refreshes with the data access of the spreadsheet's owner, so everyone who opens it sees the same figures.
</Note>

### Tracing a number

Right-click any cell and choose **Trace this number** to see where it comes from. Pluvo follows the formulas back through the workbook to the live sheets and saved Tables behind them, and tells you if it also uses numbers typed in by hand. From there, **Show rows behind** opens the rows of the Table that produced the figure.

When the Assistant uses a figure from a spreadsheet in an answer, it cites the spreadsheet, the cell, and the formula, with a link to open it.

## Sharing a spreadsheet

A new spreadsheet is private to you. To give colleagues access, choose **Share** in the top bar, or from the spreadsheet's **⋯** menu in the list, and add people as **Viewer** or **Editor**. The list's **Shared with** column shows who has access.

Viewers see the spreadsheet marked **View only** and cannot change it. See **Sharing & Access** for how sharing works across Pluvo.

## Getting your work out

Choose **Export .xlsx** while you are in a spreadsheet, or **Download .xlsx** from its menu in the list, to save it as an Excel file named after the spreadsheet.

## Finding and managing spreadsheets

The **Spreadsheets** list shows the spreadsheets you have access to, with each one's name, owner, who it is shared with, and when it was created and last updated, with the most recently updated first. Search finds spreadsheets by name.

From a spreadsheet's **⋯** menu in the list you can **Share** it, **Rename** it, **Download .xlsx**, or **Delete** it. Deleting is permanent and cannot be undone. Only the person who created a spreadsheet, or an organization admin, can delete it.

<Note>
  Spreadsheets have no version history. Export a copy before making large changes to a model you rely on.
</Note>
