## What it does

Data is where you connect the systems your numbers already live in. Once a source is connected, Pluvo syncs the underlying tables, keeps them up to date, and makes them available to everything else in the platform, from your Library to the Assistant.

You can connect three kinds of sources: cloud data warehouses, accounting and finance apps, and flat files you upload yourself. Mix and match as many as you need, all in one place.

## What you can connect

From **Data**, choose to add a data source and pick from the categories below. Use the search box, or the category filters above the list, to find a connector quickly.

<Frame caption="Adding a new data source in Pluvo">
  ![The Add new data source screen showing data warehouses, direct connectors, merge connectors, custom integrations, and file upload](/images/data/add-data-source.png)
</Frame>

**Data Warehouses.** Connect directly to your warehouse to bring in data at scale: PostgreSQL, Snowflake, and Databricks.

**Direct Connectors.** Connect straight to an app's own API for a native link: Xero, Stripe, Salesforce, Acumatica, SyteLine, Harvest, Ramp, Brex, Measure, Float, and Rillet.

**Merge Connectors.** Connect through a unified integration layer that normalizes data from many systems into a consistent shape: NetSuite, Sage Intacct, QuickBooks, Salesforce, HubSpot, and Xero. These are useful when you want the same fields and structure across different source systems.

**Custom Integrations.** Bring in pre-mapped accounting data through Merge Accounting Upload.

**File Upload.** Upload a CSV or an Excel workbook, or add a **Freeform Text** source when the context you want to share is written rather than tabular.

<Note>
  Some systems appear under more than one category, for example Xero and Salesforce. A direct connector links to that app's native API, while a merge connector standardizes its data alongside your other systems. Pick whichever fits how you want the data structured.
</Note>

<Note>
  New QuickBooks connections are made through **Merge Connectors**. If you already have a direct QuickBooks connection it keeps working as it is, and you can still reconnect it.
</Note>

<Note>
  Not every connector is switched on for every organization. If you expect to see one and it is not there, contact your account team.
</Note>

## Uploading files

**File Upload** takes CSV files and Excel workbooks, in `.csv`, `.xlsx`, and `.xls` formats, and you can bring in several at once.

Each sheet in a workbook becomes its own table in Pluvo, and you name each one as you finish setting it up. Once connected, the source keeps a record of the workbook it came from and which sheet became which table, so you can always trace a table back to its origin. Open the connection and choose **Download original file** to get back exactly what was uploaded.

<Note>
  A very large sheet can be too big to import from Excel. If that happens, Pluvo names the sheet and asks you to export it as a CSV and upload that instead.
</Note>

<Tip>
  Structure the workbook the way you want it to appear before you upload. One clean sheet per table, with a single header row, saves cleanup later.
</Tip>

<Tip>
  Uploading a file here makes its data available to the Assistant and your Library. If you want to build a model by hand in a grid, use **Spreadsheets** instead.
</Tip>

## Adding written context with Freeform Text

Not everything useful lives in a table. **Freeform Text** lets you bring in written material, such as policies, organizational context, or commentary on a period.

Pluvo reads the document and pulls out the individual facts it contains, then you review and approve those facts before they inform any answers. Only the facts you approve are used, so nothing goes into your results without a person confirming it first.

## How to connect a source

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose a source">
    Open **Data**, choose to add a data source, and pick the system you want to connect. Search or filter by category to find it quickly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Authenticate or enter credentials">
    For apps like Xero and Stripe, you sign in through that service and authorize Pluvo. Some, like Rillet, ask for an API key instead. For warehouses, you enter your connection details, such as host, database, and credentials. For files, you select the CSV or workbook to upload. Pluvo only reads your data, and never writes back to the source without your explicit permission.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the data to bring in">
    Choose which tables or datasets you want Pluvo to sync from the source.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let the first sync run">
    Pluvo pulls in the selected data. You can watch progress as each table syncs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name and finish">
    Pluvo suggests a name based on the entity the connection covers, so two connections of the same type are easy to tell apart. Keep it or rename it, then finish. It appears in your list of sources with its current status, and you can rename it later from the connection's settings.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Keeping data current

After the first sync, Pluvo keeps your data up to date by pulling in new and changed records. A connection shows **Active** when it is healthy and available to use across the platform. While it syncs, it shows how many tables are done out of the total, then **Finishing up** before returning to Active. Use **Sync now** to pull fresh data at any time, **Test connection** to check it is still healthy, and if something looks off you can re-run a full sync to reimport everything from scratch.

Open a connection to see each table's own status: **Synced**, **Syncing**, or **Queued**. Uncheck a table to stop syncing it, as long as at least one stays selected.

If you stop a sync partway, the connection is marked as cancelled rather than failed. Sync it again when you are ready, since it needs a complete run before the data can be used.

### When a table is unavailable

For warehouse connections, a table marked **Unavailable** is one Pluvo can no longer reach, usually because it was dropped from the warehouse or the connection's credentials lost access to it. Pluvo stops retrying it, and hovering the badge tells you which table is affected. Restore access to it, or deselect it, to clear the status.

That is different from **Error**, which means a sync attempt failed and is worth retrying.

## Tips

<Tip>
  If you connect the same system twice, for example two Xero entities, Pluvo names each connection after the entity it covers so you can tell them apart at a glance. Rename them if your own naming is clearer.
</Tip>

<Tip>
  Open a connection to see everything about it in one place: its status, when it last synced, the tables it brought in, and its settings.
</Tip>

## What's next

Once your data is connected, head to the **Library** to review and approve what Pluvo has learned about it, so answers rest on definitions you have governed.
