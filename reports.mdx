## What it does

A report is the deliverable version of an answer. Instead of a chat transcript, you get a titled document with sections, tables, charts, and callouts, where the figures are the same traceable numbers you saw in the conversation.

Reports are built by the **Assistant**, then live in **Reports**, where you can read them, publish a version for everyone to see, and step back through their history.

## Creating a report

Reports are created from a chat, so start in the **Assistant** and ask for one, for example "Build a monthly expense report by department."

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose how to start">
    The Assistant asks whether it should draft the report for you or give you a blank page to build up section by section.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say who it is for">
    If you asked for a draft, you pick the audience, such as CFO and finance leadership, the board, cost-center owners, or your finance team, or describe the audience in your own words. The proposed sections adapt to your choice.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm the sections">
    You get a checklist of proposed sections with a one-line description each. Tick the ones you want, add up to four of your own, then choose **Draft the report**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch it get written">
    The report opens in a panel beside the chat and fills in one section at a time, so you can read it as it comes together.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  A report can hold up to 12 sections. If you confirm more than that, the Assistant tells you which ones it left out so you can add them afterwards.
</Note>

## Editing a report

Editing happens by asking in the chat. You can have the Assistant rewrite, add, remove, or reorder a section, and adjust an individual paragraph, table, or chart. It only removes something if you explicitly ask.

Your edits apply to the draft. If the report is already published, the published version stays exactly as it was until you publish again.

## Publishing and version history

**Publish** pins the current version as the shared snapshot everyone sees. The report itself stays a live draft you can keep working on, and when the draft has moved on you can **Re-publish** to update what others see. You can also **Unpublish**, **Archive** a report you are finished with, or delete one you no longer need.

**History** opens the full version list. Each entry shows what changed and whether it came from you or the Assistant, and you can **Preview** an earlier version read-only or **Restore** it.

## Sharing and exporting

**Share** a report with specific people to give them access, and they are notified when you do.

**Export** produces a PDF of the report, which is the simplest way to send it to someone outside Pluvo or attach it to a board pack.

## Finding your reports

The **Reports** list shows the title, status, period, audience, and when each was last updated, and you can filter by status. A report moves through states as it is built: drafting while it is being written, then a draft you can edit, marked as updating while an edit is in flight, and shown as published once you have pinned a version.

## What every report includes

Reports end with a verification section that Pluvo maintains for you. It records how many figures were verified against source data, calls out any that could not be matched to a verified source, and notes when the report was generated. Figures in the document are clickable, and the conversation the report came from is where you drill all the way into a number.

## Tips

<Tip>
  Give the Assistant the period and the audience up front. It shapes both the sections you are offered and how the writing is pitched.
</Tip>

<Tip>
  Publish once the numbers are settled, then keep editing freely. Nobody sees your in-progress changes until you re-publish.
</Tip>
